Transcription

by Ben Lerner

Description & Thoughts

✈ Sangre de Cristo, NM34,000 ft

I read Ben Lerner’s Transcription mid-flight, mid-June, high above the American Southwest. In the book, the main protagonist drops his phone into the sink. The broken device leaves him with an unexpected serenity, a sense of calmness which is lost when you’re constantly looking at a phone. It was nice looking out the window and noticing the boundless blue sky, and experiencing that same stillness myself in the plane. The moment was made perfect by Spotify shuffle playing the instrumental “Kuura” by Claudio Constantini.

Before I got around to writing this, I also read explorator.dev’s post on smart phone addiction, or being addicted to the dopamine hit of stumbling upon something interesting to read. For me, that is Reddit and Hackerrank. The author terms it an addiction to information, not smartphones, but the thing is, I’m not sure how much of the stream of information I am reading is actually being retained. True retention comes by actually thinking about the text you just read/listened to, and letting it marinate your mind. Instead, we scan a few headlines, skim the top comments, and absorb other people’s opinions without looking at the content ourselves. Without the pause, it’s all just noise.

Oh, and I tracked the flights’ coordinates and elevation through the inflight wifi’s API. AI is useful for making stupid things like the visualizer you see on the left which lets you follow along the on my journey/ feel the same experience as me with the song embed and the flight visualizer.

I got sidetracked and wrote a lot of tangential stuff instead of about this book. I liked parts of it, the first part specifically, the later parts were a bit too dry. So even though the book was just a 130 pages, it still felt like a drag to finish.
